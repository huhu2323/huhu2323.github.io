# The AI Paradox: A Developer's Take on the "Builder" Revolution

AI has always been a controversial topic for me. Some people hate it; others love it. At my company, we’re diving headfirst into this shift. We aren't called "developers" anymore; we are "builders." The focus has shifted from writing syntax to understanding system architecture. Management even encourages us to use AI assistants like Claude to "dive" right into solutions without manually reading every line of code.

Here is my take on this rapid transformation.



### The Upside: The Ultimate Onboarding Tool

I love AI for what it does for my daily work. As a returnee moving into a new department, I had forgotten much of how our specific systems operated. Despite my seniority, the knowledge gap was real. AI has been a lifeline. It helps me navigate complex tasks instantly, making me feel like I already know everything. It bridges the gap between my past experience and current requirements, allowing me to function as a senior developer again, faster than I thought possible.



### The Downside: The Job Market Squeeze

However, my perspective shifts when I look at the industry at large. I believe the current AI boom might be leading toward a difficult future for the IT sector. When I was laid off recently, the job market had changed drastically. Before the AI boom, I could get hired almost immediately. This time, despite my experience, I received very few interviews.

It feels like the industry is shrinking for those who haven't adapted. If you don't learn to leverage AI, you risk being left behind. It’s not just a tool; it’s a gatekeeper.



### The Bubble vs. The Future

There is endless debate about whether AI is a bubble or the future. For me, the answer is simple: **It is a mandatory skill**. To keep your job, you must know how to use it. Ignoring it is no longer an option.



### Critical Thinking Remains Key

Looking ahead, I worry that reliance on AI could erode critical thinking skills. This is why learning the basics is still non-negotiable. 

Think of it like learning to drive a car. You can learn how to drive without knowing how the engine works, and you can certainly get a job as a driver. But if the car breaks down, the person who understands the mechanics has the advantage. In software, AI might drive the car, but we still need to know how the engine works to fix it when things go wrong.



**The bottom line**: Embrace AI, but never stop learning the fundamentals.
