title: Missing the OLD TIMES

There is a distinct, almost nostalgic ache in missing the raw, unassisted struggle of programming before AI became our constant companion. Since earning my BSIT and officially stepping into the role of a developer in 2019, I remember a time when every line of code was a deliberate choice, born from deep analysis and genuine problem-solving rather than a prompt response. We spent hours tracing logic flows, wrestling with cryptic error messages, and feeling the profound satisfaction of building a solution from the ground up with our own hands. That era demanded a level of intellectual grit and creativity that felt incredibly rewarding; we didn't just assemble code, we truly understood the architecture because we had to construct every beam ourselves.

Back then, the development process was a slow, deliberate meditation. When I stared at a blank screen, the silence wasn't empty; it was a canvas waiting for my own ideas to fill it. I remember the frustration of a bug that wouldn't budge, forcing me to re-read documentation, visualize data structures in my mind, and deconstruct the problem until I found the root cause. That struggle wasn't an obstacle; it was the very mechanism of learning. By the time I finally got the code to run, I didn't just know *that* it worked; I understood exactly *why* it worked, down to the lowest level of execution. That deep, internalized knowledge was the foundation of my confidence as an engineer.

We didn't have a "copilot" to suggest a fix or generate a boilerplate function. If we needed a complex algorithm, we had to research it, understand its mathematical underpinnings, and implement it from scratch. If we needed to integrate an API, we had to read the entire documentation, understand the authentication flow, and handle the edge cases ourselves. This manual process forced us to become better problem-solvers. We developed a mental model of the entire system because we were the ones who built it piece by piece. The code felt alive because it was an extension of our own thought processes, shaped by our unique perspective and the specific challenges we faced.

Now, with AI tools at our fingertips, development has become incredibly efficient. We can generate entire modules in seconds, debug complex issues instantly, and prototype ideas at a velocity that was previously unimaginable. But there is a cost to this speed. When the AI writes the code, we often skip the part where we truly understand the logic. We become more like reviewers than creators, trusting the machine to get the details right without fully grasping the underlying mechanics. The "aha!" moment of solving a difficult problem on your own is replaced by the quick satisfaction of an AI suggestion that "just works," but leaves us with a superficial understanding of the solution.

I miss the feeling of sitting at my desk late at night, the room quiet except for the hum of my computer and the rhythmic clacking of my keyboard. I miss the mental workout of analyzing a vague requirement, breaking it down into manageable components, and translating my thoughts into a working application. I miss the pride of knowing that every single line of code in my project was written by me, reflecting my own logic, my own style, and my own problem-solving approach. That era shaped me into the developer I am today, not just in terms of technical skill, but in terms of resilience, creativity, and deep understanding. While I appreciate the power of modern tools, I find myself longing for those days when the only voice in the room was my own, guiding the logic through trial, error, and eventual triumph.
