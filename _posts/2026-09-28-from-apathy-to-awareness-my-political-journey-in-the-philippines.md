title: "From Apathy to Awareness: My Political Journey in the Philippines"

The Philippines is currently facing a convergence of crises: rising gas prices driven by the war in the Middle East, droughts exacerbated by El Niño, political instability, and a declining peso against the dollar. These challenges have forced many of us to confront uncomfortable truths about our nation’s trajectory.

Four years ago, I was entirely apolitical. I had just landed my first job in 2019 and was busy exploring the world—experiencing freedoms and opportunities I had never known during my childhood. My family wasn’t wealthy, but my parents ensured our basic needs were met. Life was simple, and politics felt like a distant concern.

That changed when I began to understand the depth of the Philippines’ debt. Initially, I blamed the pandemic, but I soon realized that many other countries managed their crises differently. The Philippines seemed to be suffering disproportionately. Driven by curiosity, I began researching the legacy of the Duterte administration. What I found was disheartening: a stagnant economy and a nation weighed down by financial mismanagement. My admiration turned into frustration, and eventually, anger.

It’s important to acknowledge that I voted for Duterte in 2016. Why? At the time, he was a newcomer with a distinct, unconventional style, standing in contrast to established politicians like Mar Roxas, who had long been in public service but seemed to have lacked tangible achievements. I was young, idealistic, and looking for change.

During Duterte’s presidency, I remained disengaged, focused on school and personal growth. I matured slowly, and perhaps naively, I believed that former President PNOY’s legacy had made life in the Philippines comfortable enough that we could overlook deeper issues, even poverty.

However, entering the workforce shifted my perspective. I met colleagues who were politically engaged and deeply concerned about our country. They helped me understand what it means to stand for justice and for the Philippines. Through their influence, I began to educate myself on human rights, the constitution, and economic policy. I realized that the actions of the Duterte administration were causing long-term damage to our nation.

At that time, many of my friends and family members supported the Dutertes (DDS). To preserve those relationships, I kept my dissenting views hidden. I didn’t want conflict, so I stayed silent.

Then came 2025. The political rift between Presidents Marcos and Duterte created further instability, worsening the country’s problems. My frustration reached a breaking point. I felt furious—not just at the leaders, but at the complacency of those who continued to support leaders who, in my view, had failed the nation.

I became bolder, driven by necessity. I was laid off from a job I loved, and in that moment of vulnerability, I stopped hiding my views. You can call me late to the game for not being politically aware sooner, but I prefer to think of it as becoming aware when it truly mattered—when I could no longer afford to ignore the reality of our situation.

The turning point came when I shared a post criticizing Vice President Sara Duterte. A staunch DDS supporter commented, attacking me and even baselessly accusing me of drug use. That was the moment I lost my patience. I realized that silence was no longer an option. I became a vocal critic of both the Duterte and Marcos administrations.

Since then, I have criticized these leaders publicly, and yes, I have lost friends along the way. But I do not regret it. If supporting leaders accused of human rights violations defines a friendship, then those were not true friends. Their support says more about their character than mine. I have learned to walk away from relationships that compromise my values.

I may have woken up late to the political reality of our country, but I am awake now. And I will not look away.
